E-commerce Platform Redesign

UOL Host's solutions hub includes a B2B SaaS e-commerce platform for SMB sellers. A partner's legacy version migrated into it, bringing its users and its broken onboarding: 23 steps, no mobile, no clear way in. I redesigned the full journey across product, CRM, and support in 3 months.

The brief I was handed: redesign the onboarding.

The brief I shipped: four workstreams, none of them assigned to me.

Problem

23 steps before a seller could start

The setup wizard alone ran across 5 disconnected screens. Competitors averaged 6 to 16 steps. The platform was desktop-only, though most SMB owners run their business from their phones.

Constraint

The entry was off limits

The first onboarding steps were shared across the hub and owned by another team. I couldn't touch them. So I reframed the problem: if the entry can't change, how do we get users to value faster once they're inside?

23 actions vs the competitor's 6.

My Role

Evidence earned the scope.

I was the only designer across four workstreams. The CRM emails, mobile-first approach, and in-product support form were my additions, each backed by evidence: drop-off data, the 23-step benchmark, user complaints. Bi-weekly live work-in-progress reviews with 10 stakeholders across engineering, business, product, CRM, and sales turned the most skeptical voices into champions by launch.

Design Decisions

Three decisions that shaped the whole project.

01

Mobile first

SMB owners check orders and answer customers from their phones, and the legacy platform ignored that. I designed mobile first and scaled up, so every screen started from the smallest viewport.

02

Single page over multi-step wizard

Users lost context between steps, so I consolidated the wizard into one page with progressive disclosure. Engineering already knew the patterns, so the build stayed on schedule.

03

Dashboard as orientation

The first thing users said after onboarding was "what do I do now?" The dashboard answers it: what's set up, what's missing, the fastest path to store-ready. The heatmap confirmed it.

Results & Impact

Four workstreams, one launch.

All four workstreams launched together, and none would have moved the numbers alone.

Key Learnings

What this project taught me

  • Constraints focus decisions. Not being able to touch the shared entry forced me to concentrate on what I could influence, before any screen was drawn.
  • The smallest screen is the clearest test. Starting mobile-first made every page justify its content. What didn't survive the small screen probably didn't need to be there.

What I'd do differently

Research that challenges, not confirms.

I'd let user research challenge the brief earlier, not confirm the design later. One usability round changed things; more rounds, earlier, would have sharpened more decisions.
