Agência People

Women@ Leadership Day Latam

Facebook supplied the theme, logo and brandbook. I turned them into a two-day event, and ran it with the client through five months of approvals.

01

Stage

The curve wraps the key visual around the room, so the shapes read as architecture, not a projected slide.

02

Key visual

Facebook supplied the elements. I composed the key visual: shapes clustered right and bleeding off the frame, the left third left quiet for type. Every piece that follows adapts this base.

03

Accreditation

3D render
As built

I proposed the How to Fascinate personality test as the structure of the day. Its seven archetypes became the colors of everything that followed, starting with four flat-color modules where credentials were picked up.

04

Uniform

I designed the uniform: one muted pink jumpsuit, a key visual shape on the shoulder, a line drawing at the hem. Against four saturated walls, the single soft tone keeps the team readable as a group and leaves the color to the scenography.

05

Pins and credential

Shape carries the hierarchy, color carries the archetype: round primary, square secondary, same seven colors. On the lanyard, the pair reads at a glance. 675 + 675 pins, 330 credentials.

06

Archetype table

Primary and secondary archetypes crossed on one grid, in the pin colors, so any pin pair maps straight to one cell.

07

Graffiti wall

My idea. I hired Projeto Quixote, which teaches graffiti to vulnerable children and teenagers. Attendees wrote over the shapes.

08

Closing gift

Kaftans by Rede Asta, made from fabric donated by large brands. Like the wall, the gift came from the third sector. Both were my idea, and I hired both.

Art direction: Yuki Nakamura · Scenography: with Everson Cabideli (architecture) · 3D renders: Adriano Saul · Shown with the approval of Agência People.

Role

Art Direction & Design

Client

Brasil a Gosto restaurant, chef Ana Luiza Trajano

Brasil a Gosto

The chef always had something new: a menu, an event, a pop-up. I commissioned one illustration, a flowering yellow ipê, from Ingrid Pereira and built the identity around it, so every new thing could be made from the same parts.

01

The tree tells the season

When the restaurant closed for renovation, the tree told it: bare branches at the closing, in bloom for the reopening.

The same idea, by email: “A new cycle is beginning.” Then: “Come taste the new.”

02

Facade

The tree left the page: the chef had it painted on the restaurant's facade.

03

Around the table

Apron, bill folder, the Friday menu: the same tree, cropped to fit each object.

04

Boteco Brasil a Gosto

For a summer of Sunday boteco nights I made the logo and every piece: menus, coasters, matchboxes, the bottle label.

05

Brasil na Rua

The food truck needed a street voice: a rougher, stamped logo, and the ipê climbing the back of the van. On the street, the same system reached the chalkboard menu.

06

Regional menus

Each regional festival got its own menu, one state at a time, read like a field journal: the ingredients, the people, the place. Photography supplied by the restaurant; I designed the layout.

Illustration: Ingrid Pereira · Art direction, identity and applications: Yuki Nakamura.
